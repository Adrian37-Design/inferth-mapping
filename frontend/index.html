Help & FAQ

How the platform, trackers, alerts and billing work. Written for fleet owners, managers and drivers.

Getting started
What is Inferth Mapping?

A web-based fleet tracking platform for Zimbabwe and the region: live vehicle map, route history, geofences, alert rules and remote engine cut-off where the relay has been fitted.

How do I get an account?

Fleet owners request access through the website. A manager then adds the rest of the team from the Team tab, and each person signs in with their own email and password.

How do I add a vehicle?

Open Vehicles and choose Add vehicle. Enter the tracker IMEI, then a name, plate and fuel type so reports stay readable. The vehicle appears on the map as soon as the tracker sends its first GPS packet.

Where do I find the tracker IMEI?

It is the 15-digit number printed on the tracker label, and most devices will also reply with it by SMS when you send the word IMEI from the tracker’s SIM card.

How long before a newly fitted tracker shows up?

Usually one to five minutes once the unit has power, a GPS fix and a data session. A cold first fix can take up to ten minutes outdoors; basements and metal warehouses may never get a fix.

What can managers and drivers see?

Managers see every vehicle in the company, team access, alerts, reports and billing. Drivers see their assigned vehicle, its events and their own scorecard. Items outside a role are hidden rather than disabled.

Do I need to install an app?

No. The platform runs in the browser on a phone, tablet or desktop. On a phone you can use the browser’s Add to Home Screen option to get an app-like icon.

I cannot sign in

Use the password reset link on the sign-in page. If the account is disabled, or the team has changed, ask your fleet manager to reinstate or re-invite the user from the Team tab.

Trackers & installation
Which trackers are supported?

All trackers are supported. The platform already reads the common GT06, Sinotrack, Teltonika and H02 families in 2G and 4G, and any other unit can be onboarded by pointing its server address and port at ours. If a model reports in a protocol we have not seen before, send us the model and IMEI and we will add it.

Can I use a tracker I already own?

Yes. Any unit can be onboarded, whatever the brand or age. Send us the model and IMEI and we will confirm the setup; existing devices usually only need the server address and port changed to ours.

Is the tracker included in the plan?

No. Monthly plans, the once-off pass and the prepaid wallet all cover platform access only. The tracker unit, wiring and installation are quoted separately.

Does the tracker need its own SIM and airtime?

Yes. The tracker sends position data over the mobile network, so it needs a data-enabled SIM. Airtime is roughly $1–$3 per month, paid directly to Econet or NetOne and never through us.

What does installation involve?

An auto-electrician hardwires the unit to the vehicle’s 12V or 24V supply and connects the ignition sense wire so trips start and stop correctly. If remote engine cut-off is wanted, a relay is fitted at the same time.

How does remote engine cut-off work?

The command switches the fitted relay, which interrupts the starter or fuel circuit. It only works once the relay has been installed. Use it on a stationary vehicle for a theft response or recovery, never on a moving vehicle, and note that every command is recorded with the user and time.

My tracker failed or was replaced. Do I pay again?

No. The pass or plan is re-linked to the new unit’s IMEI at no charge. You pay for the replacement hardware, not for access a second time.

Can a tracker be moved to another vehicle?

Yes. Reassign it to the other vehicle and update the plate and fuel type. The existing history stays with the vehicle record it was recorded under.

Does tracking work outside Zimbabwe?

The platform itself works anywhere. Whether the tracker keeps reporting depends on the SIM roaming agreement with the network operator, so cross-border routes need a roaming-enabled SIM.

Live map & vehicles
How often does the map update?

As often as the tracker reports, which is commonly every 10 to 30 seconds while moving. Parked vehicles with the ignition off usually report less often to save data.

What does the Offline status mean?

No packet has arrived within the expected window. The usual causes are lost power, a weak mobile signal, or a SIM with no airtime. The last known position is kept on the map and labelled with the time it was recorded.

Why does the reported speed differ from the dashboard?

The platform uses GPS speed, which can differ slightly from wheel speed and lags by a second or two. Impossible readings are filtered: speeds above 200 km/h and position jumps that would need more than 250 km/h are rejected rather than stored.

Why did a trip start at 0,0 on the map?

That is an invalid GPS fix reported by the unit before it locked satellites. Those points are rejected by the platform, so the vehicle stays at its last valid position instead.

Why is fuel level empty for some vehicles?

Fuel level needs a fuel sensor or a CAN/OBD feed from the vehicle. Without that hardware the platform records position, speed and ignition only, and leaves fuel fields blank.

How do I set the fuel type used in cost figures?

Open the vehicle, edit it and choose diesel or petrol. Cost estimates use that fuel and the regional reference price for the vehicle’s last known location.

Route history & reports
How long is history kept?

30 days on the Starter plan, with longer retention on higher plans. Export anything you need to keep beyond that window.

How do I replay a trip?

Open History, choose the vehicle and the date range, then use the play, pause and speed controls under the map to step through the route.

Can I export history?

Yes. Reports export the selected vehicle and date range as CSV, Excel or PDF. CSV is the best choice if you want to work with the data further.

Why does a trip start late or split in two?

Trips follow the ignition signal, so they begin when ignition is switched on and end when it goes off. A break in mobile coverage can split one journey into two records.

Why is platform mileage different from the odometer?

Platform mileage is the sum of GPS distance between recorded points, which ignores tyre-size and odometer error. Odometer readings appear only where the vehicle supplies them over CAN/OBD.

Which reports are available?

Usage and distance summaries, trip detail, alert history and fuel figures where a sensor is fitted. Choose a vehicle, a report type and a date range, then run it.

Geofences & alerts
How many geofences can I create?

Five on the Starter plan and more on higher plans. Zones are drawn per vehicle, not shared across the whole fleet.

How do I draw a geofence?

Open Geofences, pick the vehicle, then draw a polygon or rectangle on the map. Give the zone a name and colour, choose whether entry, exit or both should raise an alert, and save.

Where do alerts arrive?

In the alert feed inside the platform, and by email, SMS or WhatsApp when a channel and contact are set on the rule. Messaging channels are subject to fair use.

An alert did not arrive

Check three things: the rule is enabled, the notify channel and contact are filled in, and the event actually occurred in the alert history. If it is in the history but not on your phone, the contact details are the usual cause.

What is a power-cut alert?

The tracker’s internal battery takes over when main power is cut, and it reports the loss. That usually means the unit was unplugged or the vehicle battery was disconnected, so it is worth treating as tampering until proven otherwise.

Can several people receive alerts?

Each rule carries its own contact. To notify more than one person, set the rule to the in-app feed so every manager sees it, and use the messaging channel for the person who must act.

A parked vehicle keeps tripping a geofence

GPS drift at a boundary can move a stationary vehicle by a few metres. Redraw the zone with a wider margin around the depot or yard, or set the rule to exit only.

Drivers & insights
What does the Insights tab show?

Events that need a decision: unusual fuel loss, unscheduled night movement, excessive idling, harsh braking, service due, and driver scorecards. Each card carries the evidence recorded for the event and the action that normally follows.

How is the driver score calculated?

From recorded harsh braking and acceleration, speeding and idling, weighted against the distance the vehicle covered. A score is only meaningful once there is enough recorded driving to compare.

How does fuel siphoning detection work?

The platform compares fuel level drops against the distance travelled. A sustained drop while the ignition is off and the vehicle is stationary is flagged as possible siphoning. It needs a working fuel sensor or CAN feed, and a false alarm is possible when fuel sloshes on a slope, so confirm before acting.

What counts as excessive idling?

More than about two minutes stationary with the engine running. Modern fuel-injected engines use less fuel restarting than idling for ten seconds or more, so switching off during loading or gate queues saves fuel and reduces engine hours.

What is the three-second following rule?

Keep at least three seconds of road between you and the vehicle ahead. When it passes a marker, count one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand, three-one-thousand before you reach the same marker. It prevents the sudden brake spikes that show up as harsh-braking events.

What should a driver do on an engine temperature warning?

Indicate left and pull off onto firm ground, switch on the hazards, select park or neutral and apply the handbrake, then switch the engine off to protect the head gasket. Do not open the radiator or coolant cap while the engine is hot. Notify the fleet manager before attempting to continue.

What driving habits reduce fuel use?

Hold a steady cruising speed rather than accelerating hard out of toll gates and junctions, avoid prolonged idling with the air conditioning running, keep tyres correctly inflated, and use engine braking rather than late braking where the road allows.

How are service reminders produced?

From distance covered since the last recorded service, measured against the service interval set for the vehicle. A reminder appears in Insights when the vehicle is close to the threshold.

Can Insights be used in a disciplinary case?

It is evidence, not a verdict. Each card links to the underlying position and fuel records, and a manager records the investigation outcome against the event. Verify the telemetry before taking action on a driver.

Plans & billing
Which plans are available?

Monthly: Starter at $12 and Professional at $17 per vehicle per month, with roughly 30% saved on yearly billing, and Enterprise priced per fleet. Once-off: a Starter Pass at $89 per vehicle for lifetime access. Prepaid: a wallet charged at $0.50 per active vehicle per day.

What exactly does the once-off lifetime pass include?

One payment of $89 per vehicle, made once. No renewal date, no debit order and no top-up prompt. It covers:

• Platform access for that vehicle with no end date: live map, trip history, geofences, alerts and the Starter feature set, for as long as the platform operates.
• Every future update, report and fix, delivered automatically at no extra cost.
• Hosting, database storage and backups.
• WhatsApp and SMS alert delivery within fair use.
• Transfer with the vehicle, because the pass is keyed to the tracker IMEI.

Paid separately: the tracker unit, installation and wiring, the tracker’s SIM airtime (about $1–$3 per month, paid to Econet or NetOne), replacement hardware after theft or damage, and any custom integration, on-site training or consulting.

How lifetime is measured: the operating life of the platform for one vehicle, not the life of the truck and not a fixed number of years. The pass does not tick down while a vehicle is parked, offline or in the workshop, and it never comes up for renewal.

Is the once-off payment refundable?

The pass activates when payment is confirmed, so once the vehicle has gone live the payment is final. If you are unsure which option fits your fleet, ask before paying and we will size it with you rather than sell the wrong one.

What if the platform ever shuts down?

Lifetime is bounded by the platform’s operating life, which is why it is sold once rather than as a short promotion. If the platform were ever wound down, active pass holders would receive advance notice and a full export of their tracking history.

Can I sell a vehicle with the pass on it?

Yes, and it is a genuine resale point. The pass follows the tracker rather than the owner, so the buyer keeps the remaining access. Send us their details and account access is moved across.

Can different vehicles use different plans?

Yes. Plans and passes are bought per vehicle, so a fleet can mix monthly, once-off and prepaid vehicles on the same account.

How do I pay?

Through Paynow, which handles EcoCash, OneMoney and Zimswitch. Amounts are quoted in USD and settlement is confirmed by Paynow before access is activated.

Do I get a receipt or invoice?

Yes. A WhatsApp confirmation and a receipt follow each successful payment, and the Billing tab keeps the record against your account for reconciliation.

How is the prepaid wallet charged?

At $0.50 per active vehicle per calendar day. Vehicles toggled inactive are not charged, which suits trucks that stand for maintenance or await parts for long periods.

What happens when the prepaid balance runs out?

The affected vehicles stop reporting until the wallet is topped up. Stored history is retained, so nothing is lost while the balance is at zero.

Can I upgrade or change plans?

Yes. Move a vehicle between monthly, once-off and prepaid at any time from the Billing tab. For Enterprise changes or fleet-wide moves, contact sales so the billing is adjusted once rather than vehicle by vehicle.

Data & security
Who owns the tracking data?

Your company does. We store and process it to run the service, and you can export it at any time from Reports.

Who can see my fleet?

Only users in your company, plus the platform administrator account that operates the service. Every request is scoped to your company, so one fleet cannot read another’s vehicles, drivers or history.

Are engine cut-off commands recorded?

Yes. The command, the user who sent it and the time are written to the audit log, so a shutdown can always be traced.

Can data be deleted?

History ages out on the retention window of your plan. If you need an account or vehicle record removed, contact us with the details so the deletion is done deliberately and confirmed back to you.

How should we protect access?

Give each person their own login rather than sharing one, remove users who leave, and keep the manager role to the people who genuinely need billing and vehicle control.

Troubleshooting & support
A vehicle has stopped updating

Check the vehicle has power, that the tracker is still connected, and that the SIM has airtime and signal. A unit that went offline immediately after a workshop visit or a battery change is usually a disconnected wire rather than a platform fault.

The map is blank or slow

Reload the page first. If the outline of the map is missing entirely, the map tiles are not loading and the device is likely on a restricted connection. Switching network or browser usually resolves it.

We get too many alerts

Raise the thresholds, set a geofence to exit only, or narrow the zone. Alert volume that nobody reads is worse than no alerts, so tune the rules to the events that actually need action.

How do I reach support?

Email sales@inferth.com with the vehicle name or IMEI and the time of the issue, or message the WhatsApp number given at signup. Including the vehicle and a timestamp means the event can be found immediately.
